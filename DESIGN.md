# VIGIL — design document

A first-person horror game for the browser (Three.js r170 + Vite, plain ES modules, no TypeScript).
Target: 20–30 minutes, one night, one house, one entity. Every asset is CC0, or generated by us.

## Premise

Your mother died on Tuesday. Her will had one strange condition: that her child
sit vigil over her body, alone, in her farmhouse, from midnight until dawn. The
instructions are pinned to the casket in her handwriting:

> Keep the candles lit. Keep the doors shut.
> If you hear me call for you — I won't. Don't come.
> It cannot move while it is seen. Keep the light on it.

It is November. It is raining. The nearest neighbour is four miles away.

## The entity ("the Lodger")

Something lived in her for the last year. It "wears people like a coat". Now
that she is dead it is out, and it wants its next coat.

**The rule (learnable and fair):** it can only move while it is *not observed*.
It counts as observed when it is in the player's view, not occluded, within
range, and **lit** (in the flashlight cone, or near a working lamp). In the dark
your eyes don't count. The flashlight battery drains, and the flashlight
flickers when the entity is near: each flicker is a chance for it to move.

- Unseen, it repositions instantly, into a new *pose*: standing, peeking round a
  doorframe, crouched, crawling, on the ceiling, reaching. You hear it move
  (skittering, joints cracking) but you never see it move.
- Seen, it stays perfectly still, apart from tiny head twitches and its breathing.
  Holding it in the light builds *dread* (grain, aberration, heartbeat, a narrowing
  FOV). At maximum dread the screen tears and the flashlight dies for a second.
- If it ends an unseen move within ~1.1 m of you, it is a jumpscare and you die.
  You retry from the last checkpoint.
- Doors slow it down. Wardrobes hide you: hold breath (Space) while it searches.

## Acts and script

0. **Midnight.** Title, then a fade in to the parlour. The casket and the body under a
   sheet; a grandfather clock; rain. The matches are on the side table.
   *Objective: Light the candles (3).*
1. **Knock.** Three slow knocks at the front door. No one there. Back in the parlour,
   one candle is out. The kitchen phone rings: breathing, then her voice
   (telephone-filtered): "Why did you let them put me in the box?" The power
   dies. *Objective: Find the flashlight (kitchen drawer), then restore the power
   (the fuse box is in the basement).*
2. **Basement.** You find a fuse on the workbench and fit it. Footsteps cross the floor
   above you and something is dragged. Then the power comes back.
3. **Empty casket.** The sheet is on the floor and the casket is empty. The front door
   is locked and the key is gone. Her diary (upstairs) explains the rule.
   *Objective: Find the key.* The hunt begins: the lights fail for good, and the
   entity is active.
4. **Upstairs and attic.** Your childhood bedroom holds the attic hook. The attic is
   a shrine of photographs of you with the faces scratched out. Her body is there.
   The key is in her hand.
5. **Run.** Taking the key wakes the hunt to full speed. You run to the front
   door, unlock it, and get outside. The ending card reads "The vigil ended at 3:12 a.m.
   Dawn was at 6:58." Then the phone rings inside the house.

## Controls

WASD to move · Mouse to look · Shift to run (loud) · C to crouch (quiet) · F for the flashlight ·
E to interact · Space to hold your breath (while hiding) · Esc to pause.

## Asset contracts

### Audio — `public/assets/audio/`
Files are `.ogg` (Vorbis, 48 kHz). The 3D sources are **mono**; the ambience and music are stereo.
`manifest.json`:
```json
{ "sounds": { "door_creak_1": { "file": "door_creak_1.ogg", "loop": false, "volume": 0.8, "spatial": true, "group": "door_creak" } } }
```
`group` lets the code pick a random variant (`audio.play('door_creak')`).
Subtitled voice lines also carry `"subtitle": "..."`.

### Creature — `src/game/entityModel.js`
```js
export async function createEntityModel(loader /* GLTFLoader */) // -> EntityModel
EntityModel = {
  object,               // THREE.Object3D, origin at the feet, faces +Z, ~2.3 m standing
  poses,                // array of pose names
  setPose(name),        // instant snap (the entity is only ever moved while unseen)
  update(dt, t, seen),  // idle micro-motion: breathing, head twitch (stronger when seen)
  setJaw(v),            // 0..1
  faceAnchor,           // Object3D at the face, used to frame the jumpscare camera
  dispose()
}
```
Poses: `stand`, `peek_left`, `peek_right` (half-hidden round a doorframe; the origin
is at the frame edge), `crouch`, `crawl`, `ceiling` (upside down; the origin is still on
the floor below it, with the body hung from y≈2.7), `reach`, `lie` (on its back, for the casket
and attic), `jumpscare` (lunging, arms wide, mouth open).

### Furniture/textures — `public/assets/models`, `public/assets/textures`
Models are `.glb`, with textures at ≤1k and embedded (and webp where possible). `manifest.json`:
```json
{ "models": { "ArmChair_01": { "file": "ArmChair_01.glb", "size": [w,h,d], "center": [x,y,z] } } }
```
`size` and `center` are the bounding box in metres, as exported (Poly Haven models are real-scale).
Textures go in `textures/<name>/{diff,nor,rough,arm}.jpg` at 1k. Their `manifest.json` lists them.
Every source and licence goes in `CREDITS.md`.
